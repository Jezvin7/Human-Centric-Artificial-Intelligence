Task 3: Learning to Defer

In this stage, both the classifier predictions and expert labels are available during training.

A separate expert competence model is trained to estimate the probability that the expert will correctly classify a given article.

For each input, the estimated expert competence is compared with the confidence of the baseline AI classifier. The system defers when the expert is estimated to be more reliable.

Task 4: Active Learning for Expert Competence Discovery

In this stage, the classifier still has access to the complete labeled AG News training dataset, but expert predictions are initially hidden.

The system can selectively query the simulated expert for individual training examples. The objective is to learn the expert's competence profile while requiring as few expert interactions as possible.

The primary active-learning strategy is pool-based competence uncertainty sampling. The system queries articles for which its current estimate of expert correctness is closest to 0.5. Random querying is used as a baseline, while a deferral-aware strategy with diversity is included as an additional comparison.

Unlike Task 3, the competence model cannot observe all expert predictions during training. Expert information becomes available only for examples explicitly selected by the query strategy.

Query Strategies

Random querying: training examples are selected randomly. This provides the baseline against which active learning is compared.

Competence uncertainty sampling: examples are prioritized when the current competence model is uncertain whether the expert will be correct. Maximum uncertainty occurs when the estimated expert correctness probability is close to 0.5.

Deferral-aware + diversity: an additional experiment prioritizes examples close to the AI-expert routing boundary and favors diverse articles to reduce redundant queries.

Uncertainty sampling is chosen as the main strategy because expert labels are expensive and the objective is to query examples that provide useful information about the expert's unknown competence.

Active Learning Analysis

At a budget of 1,000 expert queries, uncertainty sampling achieves 94.57% team accuracy, compared with 94.37% for random querying.

This corresponds to querying only approximately 0.83% of the complete training dataset while recovering 83.67% of the improvement achieved when all expert labels are available.

At larger query budgets, random querying becomes competitive. At 5,000 queries, random sampling achieves 94.97% team accuracy while uncertainty sampling achieves 94.71%.

The experiment therefore indicates that targeted uncertainty sampling is particularly useful when the expert-query budget is small or moderate. With larger budgets, random querying provides broad coverage of the relatively simple expert competence regions and can eventually perform equally well or better.
